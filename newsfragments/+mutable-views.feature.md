Views can be accessed mutably. `MemBuffer::span_mut` and `slice_mut`, `FixedMemBuffer::span_mut` and `MemArray::as_mut_slice` return a `mut` span that holds its owner mutably until its last use, and writes through it reach the owner: `let s = buf.span_mut(); s[0] = 10`. `MemSlice` gains `IndexMut`, `at_mut`, `split_at_mut` (two disjoint halves, bound `let (mut a, mut b) = s.split_at_mut(mid)`), `try_split_at_mut` (`None` past the end), the read `split_at` and `CollectionMut`, so `for mut b in s { … }` writes each element and generic code can take `mut MemSlice<T>`. A view never leaves a hole: a move out of a `mut` access of one, such as a generic `swap` called with two spans, is rejected. A projection that yields a `mut` view must hold a `mut` parameter or effect, and `var` cannot bind a `#[view]` or state-only type. `MemBuffer` gains byte indexing, `buf[i]` and `buf[i] = b`.
