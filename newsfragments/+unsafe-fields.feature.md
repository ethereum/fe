Struct and enum variant fields can be declared `unsafe` to mark values a type's safe methods trust, such as an owner's pointer and length. Initializing one in a literal, assigning it, or taking a `mut` access to it (including a `mut self` method call, a `mut` pattern binding, a loop or match that opens it mutably, or a `with` provider that a `mut` effect writes) requires an `unsafe` block or an `unsafe fn` body; reading one is safe, and an `unsafe` field may be `pub`. The pointer, length and capacity fields of `MemArray`, `MemSlice`, `MemBuffer`, `FixedMemBuffer`, `Bytes`, `DynString`, `DynArray`, `MemVec`, `DynVec` and `ByteBuffer`, and the slot of `StorPtr`, `TStorPtr` and `CalldataPtr`, are now `unsafe`, so every place core and std build or change one is marked and justified.
