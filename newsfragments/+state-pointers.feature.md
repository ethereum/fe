`StorPtr<T>`, `TStorPtr<T>` and `CalldataPtr<T>` are built-in raw pointers into storage, transient storage and calldata, as `*T` is into memory. A pointer is its address word: `slot as StorPtr<T>` makes one and `p as u256` reads it back, but a pointer never changes its space. `*p`, in an `unsafe` context, is the `T` at that address, as a place: read it, assign it, or provide it with `with (mut *p)`. Calldata is read-only. An access through a storage or transient-storage pointer needs raw state authority, such as `uses (storage: RawStorage)`, in the function's own `uses`; `unsafe` vouches for the slot but does not grant the authority. Contract fields cannot hold memory or calldata pointers, which last at most one transaction.
