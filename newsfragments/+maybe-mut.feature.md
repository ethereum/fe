Add `mut?` items: a function whose signature writes `mut?` (`fn at(mut? self, _ c: usize) -> mut? T`) is used at `ref` or at `mut`, and its body is checked once per mode. A call is at `ref` unless a use of its result asks for `mut`, such as `xs.at(i) = 1`, `let x = mut xs.at(i)` or `xs.at(i).bump()`, so a `mut?` function's result must have a `mut?` part. Trait bounds take a mode: `T: mut Index<usize>` grants a trait's `mut?` methods at `mut`, and an implementation grants it when each of them has a `mut` mode there, by a `mut?` method or by a second method of the same name taking `mut`. A `mut?` bound (`D: mut? Driver<B>`), on a function or an implementation, is `mut` where its item is used at `mut`; associated-type and `Self` bounds take `mut` (`type Item: mut Slot`). An effect may be `mut?` too (`uses (store: mut? Store)`): a call at `ref` needs only read authority, and one at `mut` needs the effect `mut`.
